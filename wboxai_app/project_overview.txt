## Project Context

I have worked on two enterprise Generative AI projects that evolved from a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system into a full Agentic AI platform. Always explain the projects from an engineering and architectural perspective rather than focusing on the business domain.

----------------------------------------
PROJECT 1: Enterprise RAG Platform
----------------------------------------

Story:
We initially built a Proof of Concept to validate whether semantic retrieval could outperform traditional keyword search over large unstructured documents. The POC proved the concept but exposed several production challenges including retrieval quality, hallucinations, latency, scalability, evaluation, and observability.

Based on these learnings, we designed and implemented an enterprise-grade RAG platform capable of reliable, grounded, and scalable retrieval.

PROJECT 2: Enterprise Agentic AI Platform
----------------------------------------

Story:

Once the RAG platform matured, we realized retrieval alone could answer questions but could not execute workflows. We evolved the architecture into a Hybrid Agentic AI Platform capable of reasoning, planning, orchestrating multiple specialized agents, invoking enterprise tools, maintaining memory, validating policies, and completing end-to-end tasks.

Rather than replacing RAG, RAG became one subsystem inside the larger Agentic AI architecture.

OVERALL PROJECT EVOLUTION
----------------------------------------

The two projects should always be presented as one continuous engineering journey:

Phase 1:
Built an enterprise-grade RAG platform to solve knowledge retrieval using hybrid search, vector databases, reranking, evaluation, and guardrails.

Phase 2:
Extended the RAG platform into a Hybrid Agentic AI system where RAG became one subsystem. Added LangGraph orchestration, specialized agents, memory, MCP-based tool execution, policy validation, and workflow automation.

Always explain the projects from an architecture-first perspective rather than emphasizing the business domain. Focus on engineering decisions, trade-offs, scalability, observability, evaluation, and production readiness.
